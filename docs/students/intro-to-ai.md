## Part 1 — What Is AI?

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)** is software that can do things that normally require human intelligence — like understanding language, recognising faces, or making decisions.

Here's the key thing: AI doesn't follow a list of rules written by a programmer. Instead, **AI learns from examples**.

### A Simple Example

Imagine you want to teach a friend to identify photos of cats and dogs.

You could write rules: *"If it has pointy ears and whiskers, it's a cat."* But what about cats without pointy ears? Or photos where the ears aren't visible?

Instead, you could show them **thousands of photos** labelled "cat" or "dog" — and let them figure out the patterns themselves.

That's roughly how AI works. Show it millions of examples, let it find patterns, and it gets really good at recognising new ones.

## Part 2 — Different Types of AI

You'll hear a lot of terms. Here's what they mean:

| Term | What It Means | Simple Example |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| **Artificial Intelligence** | Software that mimics human thinking | A game that plays chess |
| **Machine Learning** | AI that learns from data | Spotify learning your music taste |
| **Neural Network** | A type of ML inspired by how brains work | Recognising your face to unlock your phone |
| **Generative AI** | AI that creates new content (text, images, code) | ChatGPT writing a story for you |
| **Chatbot** | An AI you have conversations with | Customer service bots, or ChatGPT |

!!! tip "Don't stress about memorising these"

    What matters is that you understand the idea — AI learns from data. The specific terms will start to click naturally as you use more tools.

## Part 3 — AI in Your Everyday Life

AI is already everywhere. Let's look at some examples:

### Entertainment & Social Media
- **Netflix / YouTube / Spotify**: Recommends content based on what you've watched or listened to before
- **Instagram / TikTok**: Decides which posts and videos to show you in your feed
- **Snapchat filters**: Detects your face and adds effects in real time

### Communication
- **Autocomplete on your keyboard**: Predicts what word you'll type next
- **Google Translate**: Converts between languages
- **Gmail's Smart Reply**: Suggests short replies to emails

### Daily Life
- **Google Maps / Apple Maps**: Predicts travel times and suggests routes
- **Voice assistants (Siri, Google, Alexa)**: Understands spoken questions
- **Spam filters**: Keeps junk email out of your inbox

## Part 4 — Try a Generative AI Tool

Generative AI tools like ChatGPT can write text, answer questions, brainstorm ideas, and help you learn. Let's try one right now.

### What to Do

1. Open [ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com) in a new tab (it's free to use)
2. Create a free account, or use it without an account
3. Try asking it one of these:

   - *"Explain what machine learning is like I'm 12 years old."*
   - *"Give me 5 ideas for a school project about climate change."*
   - *"What's a good recipe using pasta, tomatoes, and cheese?"*

4. Read the response — does it make sense? Is it helpful?

!!! note "Important: AI can be wrong"

    AI tools sound very confident, but they sometimes make mistakes or even make things up. This is called "hallucination". Always check important facts from a reliable source. AI is a helper, not a replacement for your own thinking.

## Part 5 — Using AI Responsibly

AI is a powerful tool. Like all tools, how you use it matters.

### Good Ways to Use AI

- ✅ Getting explanations of topics you don't understand
- ✅ Brainstorming and getting unstuck on ideas
- ✅ Checking your writing for clarity
- ✅ Translating languages
- ✅ Learning faster by asking follow-up questions

### Things to Be Careful About

- ❌ **Submitting AI-generated work as your own** — this is dishonest and you miss out on learning
- ❌ **Trusting AI for medical, legal, or safety decisions** — always use a real expert
- ❌ **Sharing personal information** with AI tools — don't enter passwords, ID numbers, or private details
- ❌ **Believing everything it says without checking** — AI makes mistakes

### Your AI Principles

> Before you use AI for something, ask yourself:
> 
> 1. Am I being honest about how I'm using this?
> 2. Am I checking the outputs?
> 3. Am I still learning — or just copying?
