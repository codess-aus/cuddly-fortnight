## What Is Product Development?

Every app on your phone, every website you visit, and every piece of software you use was created by a team of people following some kind of product development process.

**Product development** is the process of:

1. Identifying a problem worth solving
2. Understanding who has that problem
3. Coming up with ideas to solve it
4. Building and testing those ideas
5. Releasing them into the world
6. Improving them based on feedback

The best products come from understanding real people and their real problems — not just building whatever sounds cool.

## Part 1 — The Design Thinking Process

Design thinking is a framework used by product teams around the world. It has five stages:

```
Empathise → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test
```

### 1. Empathise 🤝

Talk to the people you're trying to help. Understand their lives, problems, and frustrations. Don't assume — ask and listen.

**Example question:** "Tell me about a time you found it really hard to [do the thing your product might help with]."

### 2. Define 📌

Turn what you learned into a clear problem statement:

> **[Person type] needs a way to [do something] because [reason/insight].**

Example:  
> *"Students who commute need a way to study during travel time because their journeys feel wasted but they're too distracted to use regular study tools."*

### 3. Ideate 💡

Generate lots of ideas. Don't judge them yet — quantity matters here. You can evaluate later.

> ✏️ Set a timer for 5 minutes and write down as many ideas as you can. Even silly ones. Especially silly ones.

### 4. Prototype 🔨

Build a simple, quick version of your best idea. This doesn't have to be software — it can be:
- A drawing of how the product would look
- A paper mock-up of an interface
- A short document explaining how it works

The goal is to make your idea tangible enough to test with real people.

### 5. Test 🧪

Show your prototype to real people. Ask them to use it or react to it. Listen carefully — especially to confusion or hesitation.

**Don't explain how it works.** Let them figure it out. Where they get stuck is your most valuable information.

## Part 2 — Understanding Your Users

Every product is built for someone. Knowing who that someone is makes everything easier.

### What Is a User Persona?

A **user persona** is a fictional character that represents a real type of user. It helps a team stay focused on who they're building for.

### Example Persona

> **Name:** Aisha, 17  
> **Situation:** Year 12 student, lots of assignments, commutes 45 min each way  
> **Goal:** To use her commute time productively without feeling stressed  
> **Frustrations:** Textbooks are heavy; phone apps are too distracting; she loses track of where she's up to  
> **Quote:** *"I want to study on the train but I always end up on Instagram."*

### Create Your Own Persona

Think of a person you know (or a type of person you understand). Fill in:

- **Name and age:**
- **Situation:** What's their daily life like?
- **Goal:** What are they trying to achieve?
- **Frustrations:** What gets in the way?
- **Quote:** What might they say about their biggest problem?

!!! tip "The best personas come from real conversations"

    The most useful personas are based on real interviews with real people. Even a 10-minute chat with one person can give you insights you'd never think of on your own.

## Part 3 — From Idea to Product

Let's walk through a real example of how an idea becomes a product.

### The Idea

> *"What if there was an app that helped students find quiet study spots near them?"*

### Define the Problem

> *"Students who need to study away from home need a way to find nearby quiet, free spaces because they don't know what's available or whether it'll be suitable."*

### Ideate Features

- 📍 Map of quiet spots (libraries, cafes, parks)
- ⭐ Reviews from other students
- 🔍 Filters (WiFi, free, opens late)
- 📅 Check opening hours
- 📷 Photos of the space

### Prioritise

Not everything can be built first. Use this simple approach:

| Feature | Impact (High/Med/Low) | Effort (High/Med/Low) | Priority |
|---------|----------------------|-----------------------|----------|
| Map of quiet spots | High | Medium | ✅ Build first |
| Reviews from students | High | High | Later |
| Filters (WiFi, free) | Medium | Low | ✅ Build first |
| Opening hours | Medium | Medium | Soon |
| Photos | Low | High | Later |

**Build the high-impact, low-effort features first.** This is called the "low-hanging fruit" rule.

## Part 4 — Agile Thinking

Most modern product teams work in short cycles called **sprints** (usually 1–2 weeks). Each sprint, the team:

1. Picks the most important things to work on
2. Builds and tests them
3. Reviews what they learned
4. Plans the next sprint

This is **Agile development** — and it's much better than spending months building something before showing anyone, only to discover it's not what people actually wanted.

!!! note "Build, measure, learn"

    The best product teams don't try to be perfect on the first try. They ship something, learn from real users, and improve quickly. Done is better than perfect.

## Part 5 — Document Your Product Idea on GitHub

Let's put your product idea into a GitHub repository.

### Step by Step

1. Go to your GitHub account
2. Create a **new repository** called `my-product-idea`
3. Create a `README.md` and add:
   - The name of your product idea
   - The problem it solves
   - Who it's for (your persona)
   - 3–5 features
   - A rough sketch description (or upload an image if you have one)
4. Commit your changes

Congratulations — you've just published your first product brief! 🎉

## Key Takeaways

- Product development is a process: Empathise → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test
- Understanding your users (through personas and research) is the foundation of good products
- Prioritise features based on impact and effort — don't try to build everything at once
- Agile teams work in short cycles and improve based on real feedback
- You've now documented a product idea on GitHub
