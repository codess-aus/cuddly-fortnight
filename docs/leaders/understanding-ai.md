## Part 1 — What Is AI, Really?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is software that can perform tasks that normally require human intelligence — things like recognising images, understanding text, translating languages, or recommending what to watch next.

!!! tip "Plain-language definition"

    **AI is software trained on examples** so it can make predictions, generate content, or take actions — without being explicitly programmed for every possible situation.

### The Three Layers of AI

Think of AI as having three related but distinct layers:

| Term | What It Means | Example |
|------|---------------|---------|
| **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** | The broad field — any software mimicking human thinking | A spam filter in your email |
| **Machine Learning (ML)** | A type of AI that learns from data without explicit rules | Netflix recommending shows based on what you've watched |
| **Generative AI (GenAI)** | AI that can *create* new content: text, images, code, audio | ChatGPT writing an email draft for you |

You don't need to understand how any of these work in technical detail. What matters is knowing which type is involved in your projects so you can ask the right questions.

## Part 2 — How Does AI Learn?

Traditional software follows rules that a programmer writes:

> *"IF the email contains the word 'casino', THEN mark it as spam."*

AI software learns patterns from examples instead:

> *"Here are 10 million emails. Some are spam, some aren't. Learn to tell the difference."*

This is powerful because AI can learn to do things that are too complex or too variable to write rules for — like recognising a face in a photo, or understanding a sentence in a natural language.

### What Does "Training" Mean?

When someone says a model was "trained", they mean:

1. **Data was collected** — millions (or billions) of examples
2. **The model looked for patterns** — it adjusted its internal settings to get better at predicting the right answer
3. **The result is a model** — a file that can now make predictions on new data it's never seen before

!!! note "Why this matters to you"

    The quality of an AI system depends enormously on the data it was trained on. If that data is incomplete, outdated, or biased, the AI will reflect those problems. This is a leadership concern, not just a technical one.

## Part 3 — Where AI Is Already in Your Life

AI is not a future technology. It's already all around you:

- **Email**: Spam filters, auto-complete, smart reply
- **Search**: Google's results are ranked by AI
- **Social media**: Every feed is personalised by AI
- **Navigation**: Google Maps and Waze use AI for route prediction
- **Hiring**: Many recruitment tools use AI to screen CVs
- **Finance**: Fraud detection on your credit card
- **Healthcare**: Radiology AI that reads X-rays
- **Customer service**: Chatbots and virtual assistants

## Part 4 — Generative AI: The Current Wave

Generative AI is the kind making headlines right now. Tools like ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, DALL-E, and others can generate text, code, images, and more.

### What Generative AI Is Good At

- Drafting, summarising, and editing text
- Generating code suggestions
- Creating images from descriptions
- Translating between languages
- Answering questions (with caveats — see below)

### What Generative AI Is NOT Good At

- Being consistently factually accurate (it can "hallucinate" — make things up)
- Reasoning about very complex, multi-step logic problems
- Knowing about events after its training data cutoff
- Understanding context the way a human does

!!! note "Hallucination is a real risk"

    Generative AI can produce very confident-sounding text that is simply wrong. Always verify important facts from AI outputs. This is not a bug that will be "fixed" soon — it's a fundamental characteristic of how these systems work.

## Part 5 — Common Myths About AI

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| "AI is intelligent like a human" | AI is very good at pattern matching, not reasoning or understanding |
| "AI will replace all human jobs" | AI changes jobs; it automates specific tasks, not whole roles |
| "AI is always objective" | AI reflects the biases in its training data — often amplifying them |
| "AI just works — you plug it in" | AI systems require ongoing maintenance, monitoring, and improvement |
| "AI can do anything if you ask it right" | AI has hard limits — knowing those limits is a leadership skill |

---

## Key Takeaways

- AI is software that learns from examples, not a set of hand-written rules
- There are different types of AI — the most relevant today is Generative AI
- AI is already present in most organisations without people realising it
- The quality of AI outputs depends heavily on the quality of training data
- Knowing what AI *can't* do is just as important as knowing what it can
