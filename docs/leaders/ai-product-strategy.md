## Part 1 — Should You Even Use AI?

Not every problem needs AI. The first strategic question is always: **is AI the right tool?**

### When AI Adds Genuine Value

AI tends to add real value when:

- The task involves processing large volumes of data faster than humans can
- The patterns in the data are too complex for simple rules
- You need personalisation at scale (one experience for millions of users)
- You want to automate a repetitive, well-defined cognitive task
- The cost of being occasionally wrong is acceptable

### When AI Is Probably Not the Right Tool

- When a simple rule or database query would solve the problem just as well
- When you have very little data (AI needs lots of examples to learn from)
- When accuracy needs to be near-perfect and errors are catastrophic
- When the decision needs to be fully explainable and auditable by law
- When the problem is changing so fast the model can't keep up

!!! tip "The AI opportunity test"

    Ask yourself: *"Could a smart, experienced human do this task — if they had enough time and all the data?"* If yes, AI may be worth exploring. If no human could do it either, AI probably can't either.

## Part 2 — Building an AI Product Roadmap

An AI product roadmap follows the same principles as any product roadmap — but with extra attention to uncertainty.

### The Four Stages of an AI Product

```
Discover → Prove → Build → Operate
```

**Discover** — Identify the problem and validate that data exists to solve it.  
**Prove** — Build a rough prototype to test whether the AI approach works at all (a "proof of concept").  
**Build** — Develop, test, and refine the full product.  
**Operate** — Deploy, monitor, improve, and maintain over time.

!!! note "Most AI projects fail at 'Prove'"

    The most common mistake is skipping the proof-of-concept stage and going straight to full-scale development. Always validate the core AI assumption before investing heavily.

### Roadmap Principles

| Principle | What It Means in Practice |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Start small** | Narrow the scope; prove one thing works before expanding |
| **Define "done" clearly** | Set measurable goals for each stage |
| **Plan for monitoring** | AI systems need ongoing attention after launch |
| **Build in review points** | Revisit the direction when you hit new information |
| **Communicate uncertainty** | Tell stakeholders what you know and what you don't |

## Part 3 — Choosing the Right Approach

When your team says they're "using AI", it could mean many things. The approach matters because it affects cost, timeline, risk, and what you can and can't do.

### The Build vs. Buy vs. API Decision

| Approach | Description | Best When |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Use an existing API** | Call a third-party AI service (e.g. OpenAI, Google AI) | You need speed; data privacy concerns are manageable |
| **Fine-tune an existing model** | Take a pre-built model and adapt it to your data | You need customisation but don't have huge data volumes |
| **Build your own model** | Train a model entirely from scratch on your data | You have massive data; strict privacy needs; deep domain specificity |

Most organisations should start with option 1 or 2. Building from scratch is expensive, slow, and rarely necessary.

### Reflection Exercise

> 💭 Think about one AI initiative your organisation is working on right now. Which of the three approaches above are you using — and is that the right choice?

## Part 4 — Defining Success Metrics

*"Make the AI better"* is not a strategy. You need specific, measurable targets.

### Types of AI Metrics

**Technical metrics** — How well the model performs:

- **Accuracy**: What percentage of predictions are correct?
- **Precision**: Of the things the AI flagged as true, how many actually were?
- **Recall**: Of all the true things, how many did the AI find?
- **Latency**: How quickly does the AI respond?

**Business metrics** — What impact does the AI have on outcomes:

- Did customer satisfaction improve?
- Did processing time drop?
- Did revenue or engagement increase?
- Did we reduce errors or costs?

**Responsible AI metrics** — Is the AI behaving ethically:

- Are outcomes fair across different user groups?
- How often does the AI produce harmful or incorrect outputs?
- Are users notified when AI is influencing their experience?

!!! tip "The most important metric"

    Business metrics beat technical metrics. A model with 95% accuracy that doesn't move the needle on a business outcome is not a success.

## Part 5 — Managing Stakeholder Expectations

AI projects invite wishful thinking — from above and below. Managing expectations is a core leadership skill.

### What Stakeholders Often Believe (and the Reality)

| Expectation | Reality |
|-------------|---------|
| "AI will automate everything" | AI automates specific tasks — humans stay in the loop |
| "It'll be ready in 3 months" | AI projects routinely take longer than estimated |
| "We'll save millions" | ROI varies widely; measure carefully |
| "Once deployed, it just runs" | AI requires continuous monitoring and retraining |

### Communication Tips

- **Be honest about uncertainty** — give ranges, not point estimates
- **Celebrate experiments that fail fast** — they're cheaper than long failures
- **Frame AI as augmentation, not replacement** — this lands better with teams and public
- **Share your monitoring plan** — it builds trust that you're taking quality seriously

---

## Key Takeaways

- AI is the right tool for some problems and the wrong tool for others — always evaluate first
- Follow the Discover → Prove → Build → Operate journey; don't skip stages
- Most organisations should use APIs or fine-tuned models, not build from scratch
- Define success using both technical and business metrics
- Managing expectations — up and down — is a critical leadership responsibility
